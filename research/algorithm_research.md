## 1. Ranking Signals (What a Brand-New Account Controls)

### A. Instagram Reels
- **Primary sources:** Instagram Creators / About Instagram & Head of Instagram Adam Mosseri statements (2024–2026).
- **Top three ranking signals:**
  1. **Watch time** (completion + rewatches/loops).
  2. **Sends per reach** (DM shares divided by unique accounts reached) — Mosseri has repeatedly confirmed this is the strongest signal for **unconnected reach** (reaching non-followers, which is the only growth engine for a new page).
  3. **Likes per reach** (stronger for connected/follower reach, still meaningful for non-followers).
- **Staged distribution:** Reels are tested on a small non-follower seed batch and expanded in stages if watch time and sends/likes per reach clear stage thresholds.
- **Trial Reels:** Shows a Reel to non-followers first; requires 200+ followers to unlock (`UNVERIFIED` exact rollout eligibility threshold by region/account tier, though widely documented in Instagram's creator feature updates).
- **Hashtag limit & search:** Instagram caps posts at 5 hashtags (`UNVERIFIED` as a global permanent cap across all account types; enforce `<= 5` regardless because hashtags are a weak reach lever compared to caption/on-screen keywords).

### B. TikTok For You (FYF)
- **Primary sources:** TikTok Newsroom (*"How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou"*), TikTok Support / Recommendation Guidelines.
- **Three official signal categories:**
  1. **User Interactions (highest weight):** Predicted probability of **video completion**, rewatches, shares, likes, comments, follows, profile visits, and skips ("Not Interested"). TikTok explicitly confirms **finishing a video from start to finish** carries heavy weight.
  2. **Video Information:** Captions, hashtags, sounds, and on-screen text/topic signals used for topic matching and **TikTok Search**.
  3. **Device & Account Settings (lowest weight):** Language, country, device type.
- **New-account parity:** TikTok explicitly confirms that **neither follower count nor past viral hits are direct ranking factors**—each clip is scored on its own real-time viewer response.
- **Search ranking:** Ranks on caption keywords, hashtags, and sound/text alignment with the query.

### C. YouTube Shorts
- **Primary sources:** YouTube Creator Insider / Todd Sherman (Shorts Product Management) & Rene Ritchie (YouTube Creator Liaison), Google Support.
- **Exploration-and-expansion loop:**
  1. **Viewed vs. Swiped Away ("Stayed to watch"):** First-second stop-scroll decision is the primary gate. High instant swipe-away kills expansion.
  2. **Average Percentage Viewed (APV) / Watch duration & loops:** Finishing and rewatching short 12–20s clips pushes APV above 100%, triggering broader seed batches.
  3. **Channel-level isolation:** Rene Ritchie confirmed Shorts are evaluated **video-by-video**; a flopped Short does not put the channel in permanent "view jail."
  4. **No universal magic percentage:** YouTube does not publish a fixed "Viewed vs. Swiped Away" cutoff; benchmark each Short against your own channel median.

### Synthesis: The Two Universal Levers We Control
Across all three apps, a brand-new clipping page directly controls:
1. **Retention & looping** (stop scroll in frame 1 → finish → hard-cut loop back to teaser).
2. **Sends / shares per reach** (give viewers an obvious friend to send the clip to).

## 2. Originality, Reposting Rules & What Counts as a "Material Edit"

### A. Instagram Originality & Aggregator Policy (Expanded April 30, 2026)
- **Primary sources:** Instagram official Originality Updates (April 2024 Reels rollout + **April 30, 2026** expansion to photos/carousels; TechCrunch / Instagram Help Center).
- **Aggregator penalty:** Accounts that routinely repost third-party content without substantial transformation are removed from **non-follower recommendations** (Explore, Reels tab, Suggested Feed) on a **rolling 30-day evaluation window**.
- **Duplicate suppression:** When identical or near-identical clips exist, Instagram recommends only the original and suppresses repeat reposters.
- **What PASSES as a "Material Edit":**
  - **Unique on-screen text that adds context, a joke, commentary, or a fresh angle** (not merely describing what's visible).
  - Narrative restructuring (e.g., payoff-first recut, selective trimming, comedic pacing beats).
  - Creative overlays/graphics or original voice/commentary that change how the viewer experiences the clip.
- **What FAILS (Low-Effort / Unoriginal):**
  - Plain reposts, basic borders, simple cropping, raw speed tweaks, or foreign watermarks (TikTok/Twitch/CapCut logos).
  - **Subtitles alone + a caption credit do NOT count as original.**

### B. TikTok Unoriginal Content & FYF Eligibility
- **Primary sources:** TikTok Community Guidelines & For You Feed Eligibility Standards (`support.tiktok.com`).
- **Disqualifiers from For You:**
  - Direct reposts or clips with **third-party watermarks/UI logos** (Twitch UI, TikTok/IG watermarks, chat overlays).
  - Minimally edited screen recordings, simple flips/zooms, or merged clips without substantive creative transformation.
- **Audit path:** Always inspect **TikTok Studio** flags during daily warmup; repeated unoriginal flags in a 30-day window restrict reach and monetization.

### C. YouTube Reused Content Policy (Monetization & Distribution)
- **Primary sources:** Google Support — YouTube Channel Monetization Policies (*Reused Content*).
- **Core reviewer test:** *"Can a viewer tell there's a meaningful difference between the original video and your video?"*
- **Important warning for YouTube Shorts:** YouTube's **Reused Content** policy is stricter for **YouTube Partner Program (YPP) ad monetization** than IG/TikTok distribution rules—simple text overlays or subtitles on third-party clips often fail YPP monetization review even if they get Shorts views, and **having creator permission does NOT exempt a channel from Reused Content**.
  - *Operational implication:* Treat YouTube Shorts primarily as a **free top-of-funnel reach & brand engine** (and sponsorship/clipping-campaign inventory), and test heavier transformative commentary/editing before relying on YPP ad revenue.

## 3. Why People Share, Comment, and Follow

### A. Psychology of Sharing
- **Primary sources:**
  - **Jonah Berger & Katherine Milkman (2012),** *"What Makes Online Content Viral?"*, *Journal of Marketing Research*: Sharing is driven by **physiological arousal**, not merely positivity. **High-arousal emotions** (amusement, awe, anger, anxiety) get shared significantly more than low-arousal emotions (sadness).
  - **New York Times Customer Insight Group**, *"The Psychology of Sharing"*: People share primarily to **stay connected and nurture relationships** (78% in the study's reported breakdown) and **define themselves / show who they are** (68%).
- **Applied to GTA 6 clipping:**
  - Meme sending is **"bro this is you"** social currency—not passive news consumption.
  - Relatable chaos, absurd NPC/cop moments, and instant karma pass the **Send Test** far better than generic "cool gameplay" or raw streamer highlights.
  - `UNVERIFIED` *(from guide's 189-post winners' sample)*: Relatable clips hit 9.73% share rate, NPC dialogue 5.69%, NPC fighting back 5.09% vs. 0.72% overall sample median.

### B. Why People Comment
- **Debate & friction drive comments**, not pure jokes: bugs, absurd game physics, "was that fair?", hot takes, or relatable "what would you do?" questions.
- **Caption question penalty (`UNVERIFIED`):** Guide cites a study where ending a caption with a question correlated with **0.17x views**. Keep captions as 1–6 word statements in a friend's voice; put any honest discussion prompt in the **Instagram first comment** instead.

### C. Why People Follow
- **Expectation consistency:** Viewers follow when a profile clearly promises *more of the exact format they just enjoyed* (searchable name, matched bio, recognizable stacked layout, zero off-topic grid clutter).
- **Series labels ("Part 1") (`UNVERIFIED`):** Guide's test data suggests "Part 1" labels underperformed standalone clips—every clip must stand completely on its own.

## 4. Humor Theory Applied to Streamer Clips

- **Benign Violation Theory — A. Peter McGraw & Caleb Warren (2010), *Psychological Science*:**
  - Humor requires a **violation** (something goes hilariously wrong—a crash, cop ambush, NPC roast, instant fail) perceived simultaneously as **benign** (safe, playful).
  - **Why the real facecam is non-negotiable:** The streamer's visible shock and genuine laugh visually confirm the violation is *benign*, making the clip funny rather than just chaotic footage.
- **Psychological Distance (McGraw, Warren, Williams, & Leonard, 2012):**
  - Mild mishaps are funnier **up close** (tight facecam crop inside the border + exact, proofread subtitles).
- **Incongruity-Resolution:**
  - The hook text + setup create a confident expectation (*"HE PROMISED CHAT NO MORE CRASHING"*), and the payoff shatters it within seconds.
- **Deserved Misfortune (Instant Karma):**
  - A disaster is dramatically funnier when the streamer bragged or caused it themselves (brag → disaster within 3 seconds).
- **Contagious Laughter (Robert Provine, *Laughter: A Scientific Investigation*):**
  - Keep the streamer's real laugh clearly audible during the 0.5–1.5s reaction hold—never drown it out with background music.
- **Comedic Timing:**
  - Use at most **one** slow-mo (0.35–0.5x) or freeze frame (0.5–1.0s) right on the funniest frame, followed immediately by a sharp punchline label and a hard cut back into the teaser loop.

## 5. Cadence, Timing & Distribution Hygiene

- **Cadence (`UNVERIFIED` third-party studies cited in guide):**
  - Higher posting pace slightly reduces per-video median views while increasing total weekly views and top-10% breakout "lottery tickets" (TKMetrics 476 creators; Buffer 11.4M posts).
  - Space posts **at least 2.5 hours apart** per platform so new posts don't cannibalize the previous post's test batch.
- **Timing (`UNVERIFIED` third-party studies cited in guide):**
  - Hour/weekday explains only ~0.007% of variance (Layers, 12.7M videos), though the worst hour can cost ~21% (KASPR).
  - **WAT (UTC+1) Global Slot Mapping:**
    - `07:30 WAT` (`00:30 CT`) → UK/EU morning + West Africa morning + India noon.
    - `10:30 WAT` (`03:30 CT`) → EU mid-morning + US East Coast `04:30 AM ET` breakout window.
    - `13:30 / 16:30 / 19:30 / 22:30 / 01:30 WAT` → Covers EU afternoon, US East/West Coast daytime & prime evening.
    - **Avoid `05:30–07:00 WAT`** (`22:30–00:00 CT`, weakest historical TikTok slot in the guide's test).
- **Profile quarantine (`UNVERIFIED` operational finding from guide):** Make all profile edits in one sitting, then wait **>= 2 hours** before publishing anything to avoid zero-distribution holds.

## 6. Numbered, Testable Rules & Experiments

### Standing Production Rules (Enforced Every Run)
1. **Rule 1 — Material Edit Gate:** Every clip MUST combine (a) payoff-first teaser cut, (b) white flash + whoosh, (c) original ALL-CAPS context hook text (never describing "STREAMER DOES X"), (d) 1 punchline label on the reaction, (e) proofread burned-in subtitles, and (f) at most 1 slow-mo or freeze. *Metric:* 0 unoriginal flags in TikTok Studio / IG Account Status.
2. **Rule 2 — Send Test Gate:** Before cutting, write who a viewer would send the clip to and what they'd type (e.g., *"bro this is literally you driving"*). No friend = no post. *Metric:* Share/send rate >= **1.0%** at 24h.
3. **Rule 3 — Real Facecam & Clean Frame:** Tight facecam on top, clean gameplay bottom; zero foreign watermarks, zero Twitch chat overlays, no text over the face, no emoji in burned text. *Metric:* YouTube Shorts "Stayed to Watch" rate & IG/TT completion proxy.
4. **Rule 4 — 12–16s Sweet Spot (Hard Cap 12–20s):** Tease the end (2–4s, finishing the word/action), start full play from setup with 8+ seconds of fresh footage before payoff, hard cut on reaction peak to loop seamlessly. *Metric:* Views at 24h & like rate >= **5.0%**.
5. **Rule 5 — Caption Hygiene:** 1–6 word joke in a friend's voice with a search keyword (`gta 6`, `lucia`, `jason`, `npc`, `cops`), <= 1 emoji, no trailing `?`, credit line (`Credit: @streamer (Twitch/YouTube)`), 3–5 hashtags, never repeated within 7 days. *Metric:* Search impressions & share rate.
6. **Rule 6 — Spacing & Quarantine:** Minimum **2.5 hours** between posts on each platform; minimum **2 hours** after any profile edit; judge formats only after **24 hours**, judge posting slots only after **14 days**. *Metric:* Stuck-post rate (`<20 plays at 1h`) = **0%**.
